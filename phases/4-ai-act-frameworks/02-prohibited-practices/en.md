# The Risk Pyramid: Prohibited Practices (Art. 5) — Memorize All of Them

**Phase 4 · Lesson 02 · ~1.5h · BoK Domain II**

## EXAM FOCUS

Article 5 is pure memorization plus edge-case application — a guaranteed source of questions. You must be able to (1) list all eight prohibited practices, (2) recognize each from a scenario paraphrase, (3) know the **conditions** built into each ban (many require intent, harm, or specific contexts), and (4) recall the narrow **law-enforcement exceptions** to real-time remote biometric identification. These bans carry the top penalty tier and have applied **since 2 February 2025** — the first operative teeth of the Act.

## CORE

### The eight prohibitions — master table

| # | Practice | Key qualifying conditions | Carve-outs / notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Subliminal or purposefully manipulative/deceptive techniques** | Beyond a person's consciousness, materially distorting behavior, impairing informed decisions, causing (or reasonably likely to cause) **significant harm** | Ordinary advertising/persuasion isn't caught — the distortion + significant-harm elements are the filter |
| 2 | **Exploiting vulnerabilities** of a person/group due to **age, disability, or specific social/economic situation** | Same structure: materially distorting behavior, causing/likely to cause significant harm | Think: apps preying on children or the indebted |
| 3 | **Social scoring** — evaluating/classifying people over time based on social behavior or personal characteristics | Prohibited where the score leads to detrimental treatment in **unrelated contexts** or treatment that is **unjustified/disproportionate** | Applies to public *and* private actors; ordinary sector-specific scoring (credit risk from financial data) isn't per se banned |
| 4 | **Individual crime-risk prediction** based **solely on profiling or personality traits/characteristics** | Predicting the risk that a person will commit a criminal offence | Allowed as *support* for human assessment already grounded in objective, verifiable facts directly linked to criminal activity |
| 5 | **Untargeted scraping of facial images** from the internet or CCTV to build/expand facial-recognition databases | "Untargeted" is the operative word | The Clearview-pattern ban |
| 6 | **Emotion recognition in the workplace and education institutions** | Inferring emotions/intentions of natural persons in those two settings | Exception: **medical or safety** purposes (e.g., driver-fatigue detection); note transparency duties apply where emotion recognition is allowed elsewhere |
| 7 | **Biometric categorization** to deduce/infer **race, political opinions, trade-union membership, religious/philosophical beliefs, sex life, or sexual orientation** | Based on biometric data | Exception: lawful labeling/filtering of lawfully acquired datasets, and law-enforcement categorization done lawfully |
| 8 | **Real-time remote biometric identification (RBI) in publicly accessible spaces for law enforcement** | "Real-time" + "remote" + "public spaces" + "law-enforcement purpose" — all four elements | **Three narrow exception grounds** (below), with authorization requirements |

### The RBI law-enforcement exceptions — memorize precisely

Real-time RBI in public spaces by law enforcement is permitted **only** when strictly necessary for:
1. **Targeted search for victims** of abduction, human trafficking, or sexual exploitation, and searching for **missing persons**;
2. Preventing a **specific, substantial and imminent threat to life or physical safety**, or a genuine and present/foreseeable **threat of a terrorist attack**;
3. **Localizing/identifying a suspect** of a serious offence listed in Annex II punishable by at least **four years'** detention (e.g., terrorism, trafficking, murder, rape, armed robbery).

Procedural guardrails: **prior authorization by a judicial authority or independent administrative authority** (in urgency, within 24 hours), a fundamental-rights impact assessment, registration, national law must have opted in, notifications to authorities, and geographic/temporal/personal limits. **Post** (retrospective) RBI is *not* Art. 5-prohibited but is **high-risk** with its own safeguards — a classic exam contrast.

### Structural points the exam tests

- **Applicable since 2 Feb 2025** — before the high-risk regime; Commission guidelines on Art. 5 interpretation exist.
- Bans bind **all operators** — placing on the market, putting into service, **or use**; deployers can violate Art. 5 too.
- Penalty: the top tier — up to **€35M or 7% of global annual turnover**, whichever higher.
- Several bans have **built-in thresholds** (material distortion, significant harm, unrelated context, solely-profiling) — scenario questions turn on whether those thresholds are met.
- Overlaps: manipulative design also violates UCPD/UDAP; scraping bans echo GDPR enforcement; emotion-recognition ban interacts with workplace law.

## CASES & EXAMPLES

**Scenario 1 — The engagement engine.** A social app's recommender uses imperceptible visual flicker techniques designed to prolong compulsive use in teenagers, worsening documented sleep and mental-health harms. *Analysis:* Prohibition #1 (subliminal techniques materially distorting behavior with significant harm) and #2 (exploiting age vulnerability). Ordinary ranking to boost engagement isn't banned — the *subliminal/purposefully manipulative technique + significant harm* combination is what crosses Art. 5.

**Scenario 2 — The municipal score.** A city aggregates residents' recycling compliance, school attendance of their children, and social-media conduct into a "civic trust score" used to deprioritize housing applications. *Analysis:* Prohibition #3 — social scoring producing detrimental treatment in a context **unrelated** to where the data was generated, and disproportionate. Applies equally if a private landlord consortium ran it.

**Scenario 3 — Predictive policing edge.** (a) A police tool flags people as "likely future offenders" from personality profiles alone. (b) Another tool helps analysts assess a suspect already linked by witness statements and forensic evidence to a trafficking ring. *Analysis:* (a) is prohibited #4 (risk prediction based solely on profiling/traits). (b) is permissible support for human assessment based on objective facts of actual criminal involvement — though likely high-risk (Annex III law enforcement).

**Scenario 4 — Classroom cameras.** An ed-tech vendor sells schools a webcam system inferring students' engagement and frustration during lessons; a trucking firm uses in-cab cameras detecting driver drowsiness. *Analysis:* The school system is prohibited #6 (emotion recognition in education — "engagement" inference counts). The drowsiness system fits the **safety** exception. Swap the trucking camera to "detect employee enthusiasm for management" and it flips to prohibited.

**Scenario 5 — Stadium manhunt.** Police want live facial recognition at stadium entrances: (a) to find pickpockets; (b) to locate a specific abducted child based on a credible sighting. *Analysis:* (a) fails — petty offences aren't in the exceptions. (b) fits exception ground 1 (targeted search for an abduction victim), still requiring prior judicial/independent authorization, national-law basis, and strict scoping.

## MEMORY HOOKS

- **Mnemonic — "SVS PSEBR"** won't stick; use the story instead: *"A **S**ubliminal ad **V**ictimized the vulnerable, built a **S**ocial score, **P**redicted crimes, **S**craped faces, read **E**motions at work, **B**iometrically sorted beliefs, and watched everyone in **R**eal time."* → Subliminal manipulation · Vulnerability exploitation · Social scoring · Predictive policing (profiling-only) · Scraping faces · Emotion recognition (work/school) · Biometric categorization (sensitive traits) · Real-time RBI.
- **Two "harm-gate" bans:** manipulation & vulnerability exploitation both need *material distortion + significant harm*.
- **Two "context" bans:** social scoring (unrelated/disproportionate context) & emotion recognition (only workplace + education).
- **RBI exceptions — "Victims, Threats, Suspects":** missing/exploited persons · imminent threat/terror · serious-crime suspects (≥4-year offences) — always with prior independent authorization.
- **Real-time banned-with-exceptions; retrospective high-risk.**

**The 5 facts to retain:**
1. Eight prohibitions, applicable since **2 Feb 2025**, binding providers *and* users, top penalty tier (7%/€35M).
2. Manipulation and vulnerability-exploitation bans require material behavioral distortion causing or likely causing significant harm.
3. Social scoring is banned when it yields detrimental treatment in unrelated contexts or is unjustified/disproportionate — private actors included.
4. Emotion recognition is banned only in **work and education** (medical/safety excepted); biometric categorization is banned only for **sensitive-attribute inference**.
5. Real-time public RBI for law enforcement has exactly **three** exception grounds and demands prior judicial or independent-authority authorization.

## SELF-CHECK

1. **Q:** List the eight Art. 5 prohibited practices from memory.
   **A:** Subliminal/manipulative techniques; exploiting vulnerabilities (age, disability, socio-economic situation); social scoring; crime prediction based solely on profiling/personality; untargeted facial-image scraping for recognition databases; emotion recognition in workplaces/education; biometric categorization inferring sensitive attributes; real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces for law enforcement.

2. **Q:** What two elements elevate a persuasive AI technique into a prohibited manipulative one?
   **A:** It must materially distort a person's behavior (impairing their ability to make an informed decision, via subliminal or purposefully manipulative/deceptive techniques) **and** cause or be reasonably likely to cause significant harm.

3. **Q:** Is an AI system that predicts neighborhood-level burglary hotspots from historical incident data prohibited?
   **A:** No — the prohibition targets predicting *individual* criminal risk based *solely* on profiling or personality traits. Place-based or evidence-grounded analytical tools fall outside Art. 5 (though they may be high-risk under Annex III law enforcement).

4. **Q:** State the three exception grounds for real-time remote biometric identification and the key procedural safeguard.
   **A:** Targeted search for victims (abduction/trafficking/sexual exploitation) and missing persons; prevention of a specific, substantial, imminent threat to life/safety or foreseeable terrorist attack; locating suspects of listed serious offences punishable by ≥4 years. Safeguard: prior authorization from a judicial or independent administrative authority (24-hour urgency procedure), under enabling national law with strict scoping.

5. **Q:** A call-center uses AI to gauge customer anger on support calls, and separately to score its own agents' "positivity" for performance reviews. Which use violates Art. 5?
   **A:** The agent-scoring use — emotion recognition of employees in the workplace is prohibited (no medical/safety purpose). Emotion inference about *customers* isn't Art. 5-prohibited, though transparency duties and GDPR apply.
