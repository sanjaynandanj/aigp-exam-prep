## CORE

### What the AIGP is

The **Artificial Intelligence Governance Professional (AIGP)** is the IAPP's certification for professionals who govern AI systems — assessing risk, applying law and frameworks, and running governance programs across the AI lifecycle. It is role-agnostic: lawyers, privacy pros, auditors, product managers, and engineers all sit the same exam.

### Exam format

| Fact | Value (confirm in current handbook) |
|---|---|
| Questions | 100 multiple-choice |
| Scored vs pilot | 85 scored + 15 unscored pilot items (you cannot tell which is which) |
| Time | ~165–180 minutes |
| Question style | Fact recall + scenario-based (~30% scenarios) |
| Blueprint | BoK v2.1, four domains, effective 2026-02-02 |
| Languages | Check iapp.org for current language availability |

Two implications of the pilot items:

1. **Never burn time agonizing** over a bizarre question — it may be an unscored pilot item being field-tested.
2. **Answer everything.** There is no penalty for guessing, and 15 of your answers don't count anyway.

### Scoring

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Scale | 100–500 |
| Passing score | **300** |
| Scoring model | Scaled score — not a raw percentage; the 300 cut is set through psychometric standard-setting |

Because the score is scaled, "300 = 60%" is a myth. Do not target a raw-percentage number; target consistent performance across all four domains, since the domains are weighted differently (see Lesson 02).

### Delivery

- **Pearson VUE test centers** — in-person, the lower-friction option if a center is nearby.
- **OnVUE remote proctoring** — at home, with a webcam check-in, a clean-desk requirement, ID verification, and a room scan. Test your system with Pearson's check tool days before, not the morning of.

Either way, you book through your IAPP account, which routes you to Pearson VUE scheduling.

### Accreditation

The AIGP is accredited by **ANAB** (the ANSI National Accreditation Board) under **ISO/IEC 17024**, the international standard for personnel certification bodies. Practical meaning: the exam is developed and maintained under a formal psychometric process (job task analysis → blueprint → standard setting), which is why the BoK reads like a competency inventory rather than a syllabus.

### Fees and membership

Exam pricing, retake pricing, and IAPP membership fees change — **check iapp.org for current pricing**. What is stable at the concept level:

- There is an exam fee, typically with first-time and retake tiers.
- IAPP **membership** is separate from certification but bundles benefits (resources, member pricing) many candidates find worthwhile.
- Certification carries an ongoing **maintenance** obligation (below), which has its own fee structure.

### Maintenance: CPE

AIGP certification is not one-and-done. Like other IAPP certifications, it must be maintained through **Continuing Professional Education (CPE)** credits earned over a defined term (IAPP certifications generally run on a two-year cycle — confirm current requirements). CPEs come from conferences, web events, training, contributing content, and similar activities. Budget for this when deciding whether to certify: the credential is a subscription to staying current, which — in a field where the law changed this much between 2023 and 2026 — is arguably the point.

## CASES & EXAMPLES

1. **The remote-proctoring surprise.** A candidate books OnVUE, skips the system test, and on exam day their corporate laptop's VPN blocks the proctoring software. They forfeit the slot. Lesson: use a personal machine, run the system test early, and re-read the OnVUE rules (clean desk, no second monitor, no phones in reach).

2. **The percentage trap.** A candidate scores 290 and concludes they "got about 58% right and need 2 more questions." Wrong model — the scale is psychometric, not linear. The right response is diagnostic: which *domains* were weakest, and restudy those.

3. **The pilot-item spiral.** Mid-exam, a candidate hits a question referencing a framework they've never seen and spends nine minutes on it. It was likely a pilot item. Flag, guess, move on: at ~100 questions in ~165–180 minutes you have roughly 1.6–1.8 minutes per question on average.

## MEMORY HOOKS

**"100 – 85 – 300":** 100 questions, 85 scored, 300 to pass.

The 5 facts to retain:

1. 100 MCQs = 85 scored + 15 unscored pilot; you can't tell them apart, so answer all.
2. Pass = 300 on a 100–500 scaled score — not a raw percentage.
3. Delivery: Pearson VUE centers or OnVUE remote proctoring, booked via your IAPP account.
4. ANAB-accredited under ISO/IEC 17024 — a formally blueprinted, psychometrically maintained exam.
5. Maintenance = CPE credits on an ongoing cycle; fees and specifics live at iapp.org, not in your memory.
