# Baseline Diagnostic: 20 Questions Before You Study Anything

**Phase 0 · Lesson 04 · ~1h · BoK Domain —**

## EXAM FOCUS

No BoK mapping. This lesson is a protocol: how to take the 20-question baseline in **`practice/baseline-diagnostic.md`**, how to read the result per domain, and how to convert it into a track choice and hour re-allocation. The baseline exists to be failed informatively — a low score now is data, not judgment.

## CORE

### Why test before studying

1. **Calibration.** You cannot allocate hours (Lesson 02) without knowing your per-domain starting point.
2. **Pre-testing effect.** Attempting questions before learning the material measurably improves later retention — even when you get them wrong.
3. **A "before" photo.** Motivation compounds when Week 6 you can see Week 1 you.

### The protocol

| Step | Rule |
|---|---|
| 1. Timing | One sitting, **35 minutes** for 20 questions (~1.75 min/question — real exam pace) |
| 2. Conditions | No notes, no search, no pausing. Closed book, quiet room |
| 3. Guessing | Answer every question; mark a small `G` next to genuine guesses |
| 4. Scoring | Use the key in `practice/baseline-diagnostic.md`; score **per domain**, not just total |
| 5. Journal | Every miss goes straight into the question journal with an error type (Lesson 03) |

The `G` marks matter: a *correct guess* is still a knowledge gap. Your effective score per domain = correct answers minus lucky guesses.

### Interpreting results per domain

The baseline has 5 questions per domain. With samples this small, read bands, not decimals:

| Per-domain result (of 5) | Read it as | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 4–5 correct (no guesses) | Working strength | Skim that phase; do lessons' SELF-CHECKs only, read fully where you miss |
| 2–3 correct | Typical starting point | Follow the track as written |
| 0–1 correct | Priority gap | Add hours to that phase; schedule its lessons early in your week when you're fresh |

And read the total against track choice:

| Total (of 20) | Suggested track adjustment |
|---|---|
| 14+ | Sprint (4-week) is realistic if your calendar allows ~20 h/wk |
| 8–13 | Default (8-week) as designed |
| 0–7 | Steady (12-week), or Default with the Domain-I phases done slowly and completely |

### Typical profiles

| Background | Usual baseline shape | Usual adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Privacy/compliance (CIPP/E, CIPM) | Strong II, weak I & III | Skim Phase 3, full Phase 1, extra Phase 5 |
| Engineering/data science | Strong I, weak II & IV | Skim Phase 1, double down Phases 3–4 and 6 |
| Audit/risk | Strong IV concepts, weak I & II specifics | Full Phases 1 and 4; Phase 6 will feel familiar |
| New to both | Flat and low | Steady track, no skips — flat-low is the *easiest* profile to plan for |

### What the baseline is not

- Not a prediction of your final score — 20 questions have wide error bars.
- Not a license to skip whole phases — even a 5/5 domain gets its SELF-CHECKs and quiz.
- Not repeatable as a "progress test" — you'll remember the items. Progress is measured by the Phase 7 domain quizzes and mock.

## CASES & EXAMPLES

1. **The privacy pro.** A CIPP/E holder scores 15/20: Domain II 5/5, Domain I 2/5 ("what's the difference between a parameter and a hyperparameter?"). She takes the Sprint track but swaps the shortcut table's advice: *skims Phase 3, fully studies Phase 1.* The baseline overrode the default assumption that privacy pros can coast early.

2. **The lucky guesser.** A candidate scores 11/20 but marked `G` on 5 of the correct answers. Effective knowledge: ~6/20. He picks the Steady track instead of Default — and avoids discovering the truth in Week 6 instead of Week 1.

3. **The misread cluster.** A candidate scores 9/20, but the journal shows 6 misses were "misread the stem" (e.g., answered what a *provider* must do when the question asked about the *deployer*). Her content gap is smaller than the score implies; her Phase 7 technique work just got more important. Track: Default, unchanged.

## MEMORY HOOKS

**"35 closed-book minutes, scored by domain, every miss journaled."** That is the whole protocol in one line.

The 5 facts to retain:

1. Take the baseline *before* Phase 1 — pre-testing improves later learning and sets the hour map.
2. Real-exam pace: 35 minutes / 20 questions, closed book, no pauses.
3. Mark guesses; correct guesses are still gaps.
4. Read per-domain bands (4–5 skim / 2–3 as-written / 0–1 add hours), then total → track (14+ Sprint, 8–13 Default, 0–7 Steady).
5. The baseline is one-shot; progress is measured later by the Phase 7 quizzes and mock, never by retaking the baseline.

## SELF-CHECK

1. What are the time limit and conditions for the baseline?
2. Why mark guesses even when they turn out correct?
3. Your Domain III result is 1/5 and everything else is 3/5. What two concrete adjustments follow?
4. You scored 16/20 with no guesses. What track does that suggest, and what's the caveat?
5. Why shouldn't you retake the baseline in Week 5 to measure progress?
